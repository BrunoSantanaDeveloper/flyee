# How to use

You say what you want to sell and by when, and you get your brand's paid-traffic plan written the way an expert would write it: what a customer may cost, where the goal comes from, the offer, the calendar up to the target date and what to do first. Before asking anything, your AI reads what the project already knows; then it asks only what is missing, each question with a suggestion.

## Ask like this

- "I want a plan to start advertising my product."
- "Make a paid-traffic plan up to Black Friday. We have an old contact base and we have never advertised."
- "With R$ 4,000, can we reach 30 subscriptions by the end of the month? Build the plan and tell me what is missing."
- "Does starting now, before the date, make sense? Build the plan and guide me."
- "Redo the plan with the new budget and show me what changed."

## What happens

1. The AI reads what the project already has: your brand, the campaign briefings, the capture of your site, the Instagram research, the inventory of your base. What is there it does not ask.
2. It sends one message, with up to four questions, in the order that decides the plan: the product and the offer, who it is for and how many people you can reach, the goal and the date, the budget. Each question comes with its suggestion and what changes if your answer is different. You answer what you know; what you do not know stays a labelled assumption.
3. It does the arithmetic and shows it in a few sentences: what a customer may cost, how many may come from people who already know the brand, how many leads and visits the goal needs, whether the budget takes an ad set out of the learning phase. With a small budget and an expensive product, this changes the conversation and decides what the campaign optimizes for.
4. It shows the forms of the offer as options, points out whether a running promotion collides with the peak one and lists what an offer sold online has to carry.
5. It draws the calendar backwards: the phases, the decision point, the deadlines that are not up for debate and the holidays on the way.
6. The plan is written in the project, as a draft. In the conversation come three lines: the main finding, the first step and what you still have to decide. When you approve, the version is frozen and the other apps start reading the plan.

## What you receive

| Where | What |

| media-plan/marca.md | your brand's facts, with source and date |
| media-plan/(plan)/plano.md | the plan to read: summary, decisions, the story, the moves, what not to do, what is still unchecked |
| media-plan/(plan)/premissas.json and the arithmetic files | the premises with the origin of each one, the scenarios, the offer and the calendar |

## Tips

- The more numbers of your own (price, margin, how long a customer stays, your base's conversion), the fewer reference ranges enter the plan. Each range is labelled, with its source and date, for you to replace.
- Fill in marca.md once: the next plan starts with fewer questions.
- Ask for the section you want to see in the conversation: "show me the calendar", "show me the scenarios".
- To change an approved plan, ask for the change: the next version is born as a draft and the previous one is kept.

## Limits

- The plan does not promise results: the scenarios are arithmetic over labelled assumptions.
- It does not create campaigns, upload audiences or send messages. It prescribes; you execute, with your AI beside you.
- The holidays marked are Brazil's national ones; you add your city's.
- The lines about consumer law and data protection are method, not legal advice.
- The app speaks Brazilian Portuguese: the questions and the plan are written in Portuguese, and amounts follow the Brazilian format (R$ 1.490,00).

## Channels, structure and rules

After the arithmetic, the AI decides each channel: Meta, Google Search, e-mail and WhatsApp to your base, partners, TikTok, LinkedIn and YouTube. Each comes out as run, test, later or no, with the reason in one sentence and what would flip the verdict. A channel never enters for being new, and when the hours asked exceed the hours you have, the plan says which test leaves first.

For the channels that run or test, it writes the structure as business: on Meta, why the campaign optimizes for that event, why one ad set, how much it spends per day against the minimum the platform needs to learn, who stays out, the remarketing by stage; on Google Search, the campaign for your brand's name and the campaign for the problem people search, with the keywords, the negatives and the bid. When the ad set's budget sits below the minimum, the AI brings three exits and you choose.

The decision rules say what counts as enough data to judge an ad, when to pause, when to scale and by how much, when to swap the piece, and what is looked at every week. Five triggers (cost, account, base, product, tracking) come with a plan B each. They are the same numbers the measurement app uses to read results, so the plan and the read never disagree.

## After the first week

When the measurement app writes the weekly read, ask "redo the plan with this week's read". The next version is born as a draft with "What changed and why" on top: the findings, what worked and what did not, what the read proposes as your decision, what was not measured and the goal updated by what the spreadsheet recorded. The approved version is kept. The replan does not move money and does not change the goal in silence: both are yours.
