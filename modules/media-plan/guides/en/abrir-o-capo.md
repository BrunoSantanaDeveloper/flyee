# Under the hood

How the plan is built on the inside, for whoever wants to know where each number comes from.

## Premises have an origin

Ten premises hold the plan: product and offer, who it is for and how many, goal and date, budget, price and plans, contact base, readiness of the product and the page, optimization event, team capacity, accounts and tracking. Five of them block the approval: product and offer, who it is for, goal and date, budget, event.

Each premise carries the origin of its value. "Informed" is what you said. "Found" is what the project already had, with the file it came from. "Suggested and pending" is what the AI proposed and you have not confirmed yet; it becomes "confirmed" with your yes. The script refuses a value without an origin and a suggestion that does not say what changes if the answer is different. That is what separates an expert plan from a well-written guess: you know what is fact, what is finding and what is proposal.

## What a customer may cost

With the price, the margin and how long a customer stays, the app computes the allowable cost per customer as 12 months of payback over the margin, capped by what the customer leaves while they stay. When the margin or the retention comes from a rule of thumb, a second cap enters: a third of the first year's revenue. The lower one rules, and the plan says which.

From there, the funnel worked backwards: the goal splits between people who already know the brand (your base, at a rate you inform or a reference range) and people who have never heard of it; for these, how many leads and how many page visits are needed, and how much a lead may cost. Three scenarios cross the cost per lead with the conversion: conservative, expected and optimistic. Each says what the goal would cost and how many customers your budget buys, and flags when the number exceeds your team's capacity.

## The learning phase

Meta stabilizes an ad set with about 50 optimization events in 7 days. The app divides your daily budget by that and says how much an event may cost for the set to learn. With an expensive product and a small budget, a purchase does not get there; a lead does. That is the arithmetic that decides what the campaign optimizes for, and it appears in the plan before any creative.

## The frequency of the base

A small base is not an ad audience: the app computes how many people Meta would recognize from your list and how many times each one would see the ad if the week's budget all went to them. Above five times a week, the plan sends the base to e-mail and messages and leaves the budget for new people.

## The offer and the collision

The forms of the offer come from your price: the bonus with no discount, the annual at the price of ten months, the locked price, the discount with an end date. The app points out the collision when a running promotion ends less than 30 days before a deeper one: whoever bought earlier would pay more than whoever waited. And it lists what the law asks of an offer sold online, for you to confirm.

## The calendar, backwards

From the target date: peak, warm-up (7 days), scale (7 days), tests (what is left, in 7-day windows) and preparation. Fixed deadlines: the sequence written 14 days before, the page ready 10 days before, tracking and the offer live 7 days before, ads in review 3 days before, the launch scheduled 2 days before. A decision point 14 days before, to redistribute the budget. Brazil's national holidays enter with their source and the date they were checked.

## The written plan

The summary holds at most 150 words: what we looked at, up to three findings with the certainty of each (confirmed, likely, first look) and what to do first. Then the decisions that are yours, the story, up to six moves, what we are not doing, what we could not check and the sources. The AI writes the narrative; the script checks that it promises no result, cites the right numbers and fits the size.
